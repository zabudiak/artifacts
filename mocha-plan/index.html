Prepared Fri 9 Oct 2026 for Zaid. Sources: Mitchell Keller's X article "The six levels of working with Grok Bot" (30 Sep) and his reply on running work through a CLI; a read-only look at ~/Documents/Synteria on Mocha-3, part of it surveyed by Haiku through the Claude CLI and then spot-checked. Nothing in the workspace was changed.

1. The thread, in short

Mitchell's article sets out a ladder: one "First Mate" bot you talk to, "Second Mates" that own domains like GTM, a bench of consultant bots, routines, an always-on box, connectors to real work surfaces, and approval gates on anything that sends or spends. In a reply he adds the part that matters most for cost: Grok Bot usage counts model spend and compute, so hand routine work to a CLI on your own machine (his bot sends work to a Codex model).

Applies to youDoesn't apply (yet)

  • Budget routines by work done each week. Start with fewer and add more later.
  • Connect only what a workflow needs.
  • Let routines find work for you: proposals owed, gone-quiet leads, roster drift.
  • Ask before anything that sends or spends. You already run this way.
  • A First Mate / Second Mate hierarchy. You have one assistant and one business, and an extra layer would only add routing cost.
  • Consultant bench and "Distill Anyone" rooms.
  • Code Mode MCP gateway, VPS, Infisical. More infrastructure than you need right now.
  • Clay Desk or other paid enrichment running on its own.
  • Bookmark reviewer routine.

3. Changes, in priority order

1Send heavy reading to Haiku on the Mac

What: Any job that reads more than a few files (transcripts, roster reconciliation, workspace scans, proposal prep) runs as claude --model haiku --allowedTools "Read,Glob,Grep" -p "..." in the Synteria folder. It runs as a tracked background job and writes one short report. Mocha reads only that report and checks any claim it plans to act on.

Why: This is Mitchell's main point, and it fits your cost concern. Today's test went fine. It also showed the rule: Haiku reported two launchd jobs as broken because it missed that their paths are symlinks. They work. So verify before you act.

Effort: smallUsage: large dropNeeds the Mac awake; falls back to connectors

2Cut routine frequency and stop duplicate pings

What: Inbox watch goes from hourly 07:30 to 22:30 (16 runs a day) to four runs (08:30, 12:30, 17:30, 21:30), and stays silent unless something is new. Meeting prep goes from hourly (11 runs) to the morning brief plus one run about 90 minutes before each call. On most days that's the 19:00+ evening calls. Keep a small "already pinged" ledger of thread and event IDs so nothing gets reported twice. Re-run the routines that failed on 8 Oct once as a test, and if any fails again, read the error before changing anything.

Why: These two routines account for about 27 of the roughly 30 weekday runs and cause most of the repeat pings.

Effort: smallUsage: large drop

3Read Instantly Unibox replies through the Mac instead of pasting them

What: Inbox watch and Follow-ups call the existing read-only functions in instantly_api.py on the Mac (interested leads, then each thread) with the project venv. Haiku turns the result into a list of "new reply, who, what they asked". No send or mutate functions, ever.

Why: This removes the most manual step you have, and the code already exists. Sends stay with you through the existing numbered gate.

Effort: mediumUsage: small (runs on the Mac)Each Mac call asks for local-tool approval

4A people key, matched on email address

What: One short table: name, email, company, role (client, prospect, internal). Mocha matches people by email address or domain, never by first name alone. When a first name maps to more than one row, it uses the full name and company in every ping and draft.

Why: Mixing up people in a client draft costs trust. The workspace has at least six first-name clashes.

Effort: smallUsage: none

5Proposals owed: draft them, never send

What: Monday pipeline review and Follow-ups show one "owed" list with a deadline for each item (Hitch and Graziani before Tue 13 Oct; Ilan Dayan's objection reply; Porter Pro, LIT and Obieo nudges). For each item, Haiku drafts the proposal terms from the Fathom transcript and the client's file, in the Proposal gen format. Mocha hands you a Gmail draft, or the content for proposal-cli.ts, for you to check and send.

Why: Proposals are where revenue is stuck. Right now this list exists only in the roster text.

Effort: mediumUsage: medium, mostly on the Mac

6Call pack in meeting prep

What: For sales calls, meeting prep pulls one screen from sales_playbooks/ (framework v2, discovery playbook) and the prospect's last Fathom transcript: what they said, what you quoted, what you owe, three questions to ask. Client calls use _clients/<slug>/ and ROSTER instead.

Why: The scripts exist, but Mocha can't see them, so prep stays generic.

Effort: smallUsage: small

7Weekly roster drift check in the Friday wrap

What: Haiku compares _clients/ROSTER.md, registry.json, _state/ATTENTION.md and live Instantly campaign status, then lists the conflicts. You edit the roster. Mocha never does.

Why: N5, AdCrunch, Vector is Love and Hitch are out of sync today, and Mocha's briefs inherit whatever the roster says.

Effort: smallUsage: small

4. What not to do

  • No sending, booking or Instantly writes. Drafts only. The Follow ups gates stay the only send path.
  • No paid lookups (GetLeads, Airscale, Clay) without your yes on that specific run.
  • No writes to the Synteria workspace, including memory, the roster and Notion system rows owned by other jobs.
  • Don't rebuild what Modal already does (hot-reply cards, 08:00 digest). Read from those jobs, don't repeat them.
  • No multi-bot hierarchy, MCP gateway, VPS or new connectors until the changes above prove too small.
  • Don't trust a Haiku summary on its own. Check a claim against the source before it shows up in a ping or a draft.
